RESEARCH & METHODOLOGY

Making invisible defending visible.

A tackle or an interception is easy to notice. The defensive decision that prevents a pass from becoming dangerous is much harder to see. The Invisible Defender explores how a defender's positioning and movement can change attacking options, and turns those calculations into synchronized 2D/3D replays with evidence-grounded commentary.

What is the project trying to solve?

Traditional highlight clips reward actions that happen on the ball. Yet much of defending happens without a touch: screening a passing lane, tracking a runner, holding the defensive line, or moving at exactly the right moment. The project is a small interactive experiment in making these off-ball decisions understandable for fans, analysts and broadcasters.

The user chooses a synthetic match situation, selects a defender, and compares nine body-relative movements before or after a pass. Two views share the same simulated timeline: a 3D tactical replay and a 2D pitch diagram. A lower attacking-threat index is better for the defending side within this model.

How the model works

1. Time-aware passing lanes

Passes have a launch time, a travel path and a travel duration. Each defender also has a time-dependent trajectory. The model compares ball and defender positions at the same instant to estimate soft passing-lane coverage. A separate geometric contact check marks when a defender comes within a chosen illustrative distance of the ball.

2. Team coverage and individual contribution

Defensive coverage is evaluated both collectively and with the selected defender excluded. This allows the viewer to see whether the defender contributes beyond the areas already protected by teammates. The system also contains a simplified marking-responsibility penalty to avoid rewarding every movement toward a pass if it abandons an assigned attacker.

3. Nine counterfactual movements

The chosen defender is tested moving forward, backward, left, right, diagonally or holding position, relative to their simulated body orientation. Movement speed, reaction delay and available time are constrained; the other players follow scripted trajectories. The lowest index is simply the lowest among these nine tested options—not a claim of globally optimal play.

4. Before and after the pass

Before the pass, a defender's position may influence which passing targets appear available, including their modeled offside position at kick. After the pass, the target and offside snapshot are fixed and the evaluation focuses on reachable interceptions. Offside position is not, by itself, an offside offence.

5. Evidence-grounded AI explanation

The Python simulation computes the numeric results deterministically. Microsoft Foundry agents, when enabled, select verified evidence and turn it into a short tactical commentary. Output is checked against an evidence allowlist. If the AI service is unavailable, a deterministic explanation is used. AI does not create or alter the underlying scores.

Research that informed the design

The following work motivated key concepts. This application is an independent, simplified implementation: it does not reproduce the trained models, parameter estimates, code or validated results of the cited research.

Quantifying Off-Ball Defensive Impact through Cover ShadowsLorenzo Cascioli, Allen Wang, Luca Stradiotti, Maaike Van Roy, Pieter Robberechts, Maxim Wouters, Arne Jaspers & Jesse Davis · Hudl Performance Insights, 2025

Inspiration: the idea that occupying or obstructing a passing lane can be a measurable defensive contribution even without a tackle. This project's pass-lane coverage is a much simpler, hand-designed proxy.

Publication record
Better Prevent than Tackle: Valuing Defense in Soccer Based on Graph Neural Networks (DEFCON)Hyunsung Kim, Sangwoo Seo, Hoyoung Choi, Tom Boomstra, Jinsung Yoon & Chanyoung Park · 2025

Inspiration: evaluating how defenders change the value of an opponent's attacking options within a team context. Unlike DEFCON, this project has no trained graph neural network or learned EPV.

Beyond Expected GoalsWilliam Spearman · MIT Sloan Sports Analytics Conference, 2018

Inspiration: studying valuable off-ball positioning and time-dependent opportunities rather than only completed shots or on-ball events. Our model does not implement Spearman's probabilistic framework.

Decomposing the Immeasurable Sport: A Deep Learning Expected Possession Value Framework for SoccerJavier Fernández, Luke Bornn & Dan Cervone · MIT Sloan Sports Analytics Conference, 2019

Inspiration: separating different attacking possibilities and interpreting their tactical value in a wider team context. Our threat index is heuristic and is not a calibrated expected possession value.

Technical implementation

The backend is written in Python and FastAPI. It handles scripted situations, timed trajectories, pass-lane coverage, offside snapshots, nine counterfactual movements, and the commentary API. The interface uses JavaScript, SVG and Babylon.js for a synchronized 2D/3D tactical replay. Microsoft Foundry provides optional evidence-grounded agent commentary.

What the prototype does not claim

All match situations, player positions, velocities, body directions and tactical responsibilities are synthetic. The displayed index is an uncalibrated, hand-designed attacking-threat score: it is not xG, xT, EPV, a scoring probability, a verified interception probability or a player rating. Ball contact is an illustrative geometric event, not verified ball possession. Real-world use would require tracking data, learned/calibrated models and external validation.
